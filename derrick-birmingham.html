Elvis Costello & The Imposters — Radio Soul!: The Early Songs

Symphony Hall · Centenary Square, B1

Acelebration of Costello's first five years — My Aim Is True through Get Happy!! — performed with The Imposters and special guest Charlie Sexton on second guitar. The opening night of his UK tour, immediately after a Brighton warm-up, and the only Midlands date. This is the catalogue you'd most want to hear him play.

Why this is for Derrick Matches your interest in the original new-wave / pub-rock crossover. You went to see him at the NEC in 1983 (per your saved history). Sit-down venue, generous interval, easy to reach from Edgbaston by tram.

Jason Isbell & the 400 Unit

Symphony Hall · Centenary Square, B1

The Alabama songwriter whose work descends straight from the Townes Van Zandt / Guy Clark line — character songs about Southern lives, drinking, regret and recovery, carried by one of the finest live bands in American music. If Drive-By Truckers ever spoke to you, this is essential.

Why this is for Derrick Songwriter-led Americana, exactly the territory you've moved toward in the last decade. Symphony Hall acoustics suit the quieter material — and the venue is wheelchair-friendly throughout.

Ray LaMontagne — Trouble 20th Anniversary Tour

Symphony Hall · Centenary Square, B1

LaMontagne revisits his 2004 debut in full — the gravel-and-smoke record that pulled Van Morrison and early Cat Stevens into the 21st century. He plays the whole album end-to-end, then a second set of selections from the rest. A confessional, careful show; he doesn't talk much.

Why this is for Derrick Direct descendant of the 70s singer-songwriter tradition. Trouble has been in your saved Spotify library since 2018.

Rumours of Fleetwood Mac

Symphony Hall · Centenary Square, B1

The longest-running Fleetwood Mac tribute in the UK and the only one endorsed by Mick Fleetwood himself. A two-and-a-half hour show built around Rumours and Tusk, with deeper cuts from Tango in the Night. Not a parody — closer to a careful, loving restoration.

Why this is for Derrick Squarely in your decade. We've shown this rather than the touring Fleetwood Mac estate show because the latter is at the arena, which you've previously told us you avoid.

The Soft Parade — The Doors revisited

Hare & Hounds · Kings Heath, B14

A four-piece taking The Doors' first three albums — L.A. Woman, Strange Days, the self-titled — back to the bar-room intensity they were written in. The upstairs room at the Hare & Hounds is tiled, dark and intimate. UB40 played their first gig downstairs in 1979; the upstairs room hasn't changed much since.

Why this is for Derrick Smaller venue, but the Hare & Hounds has seating along three walls and the gig finishes by 10.30pm. Kings Heath is one stop on the train from University station.

Jimmy Aldridge & Sid Goldsmith + Wakefire

Symphony Hall, Foyle Foyer · Centenary Square, B1

A quietly devastating English folk duo whose new record draws on Wendell Berry, John Clare and the Cumbrian shepherds' songs. Support from Wakefire, an Irish-trad ensemble playing summer-themed sets. Sit-down show, foyer setting — closer to a folk club than a concert hall.

Why this is for Derrick The Irish-trad opening set is the connection here — Wakefire's fiddler is from Galway and the programme overlaps with the territory of the Chieftains-era you've listened to.

Sitting, silence, study

Five gatherings across Moseley, Edgbaston and Kings Heath — from open Saturday morning sits at the Birmingham Buddhist Centre to a Zen evening at the Vihara. None require booking unless noted.

Introduction to Meditation & Buddhism

Birmingham Buddhist Centre · 11 Park Road, Moseley, B13

A six-week evening course rooted in the Triratna tradition. Three meditations taught over the weeks, threaded through with core Buddhist teachings on awareness, kindness and the nature of experience. The Moseley centre is one of the warmest rooms in the city.

Why this is for Derrick Marked because you've attended the Centre's drop-in sessions before. This course is the natural next step — and runs in the evening, after rush-hour traffic settles.

Silent Saturday Morning Meditation

Birmingham Buddhist Centre · Moseley, B13

Three unguided sits, ringing in on the hour. You can join for any one of them, or stay for all three. The room fills with regular practitioners and a few curious visitors; bells mark the stages. No teacher, no instruction — just the held space.

Why this is for Derrick For more experienced sitters. Morning slot is gentler on the joints than evening. Tea is offered between sessions.

Zazen at the Birmingham Buddhist Vihara

Birmingham Buddhist Vihara · Osler Street, Ladywood, B16

A two-hour Zen meditation evening hosted in the Vihara's upstairs shrine room — first and third Fridays each month. Two periods of zazen with a kinhin (walking meditation) between, followed by a short dharma reading and tea. Cushions and chairs both available.

Why this is for Derrick A different tradition to Triratna — included so you can see another flavour. The Vihara is the oldest Theravada temple in the UK (founded 1962); the Zen group is an evening guest.

Amida Birmingham — Walking Meditation & Tea

All Saints Community Centre · Vicarage Road, Kings Heath, B14

A small Pureland-tradition group meeting Saturday mornings. Begins with walking meditation, followed by a seated period, then tea and open discussion of a teaching. Suitable for complete beginners and seasoned sitters alike. Around eight regulars; warm and unhurried.

Why this is for Derrick Smaller and less formal than the Centre. The walking meditation component is gentle on the back. Around the corner from where your daughter lives.

"Digging" — A Heaney Centenary Reading

The Library of Birmingham · Centenary Square, B1

An afternoon reading marking the centenary year of Seamus Heaney's birth, with poets from Birmingham and Belfast reading his work alongside their own. Hosted by VERVE Poetry Festival with the Irish Centre. Heaney's "Death of a Naturalist" read by Cahal Dallat in the final hour.

Why this is for Derrick Heaney is at the heart of your saved poetry library. Library of Birmingham is a 12-minute walk from New Street; lifts to the reading room.

The Hive · Digbeth, B5

The Hive · Digbeth, B5
